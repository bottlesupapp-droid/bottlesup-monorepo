# Completing the customer app: the plan, the decisions made, and the order of work (27 Oct 2026)

The owner asked (27 Oct) to complete the customer app **100% against the client's plan** (`BottlesUp_User_App_Plan_and_Flows.pdf`, 15 Oct) first, and only then test on real devices. This file is the single plan for that. What exists today and what each section of the PDF needs is in `apps/bottles-up-user/docs/USER_APP_PLAN_STATUS.md`; this file says **how it gets built**.

Production has no social, messaging, marketplace or growth tables, so almost everything below starts in the database. Work happens in the **website repo** (migrations, SQL tests, edge functions, the CMS), then the **customer app**, then the **vendor app** where a screen of its own is needed.

## How every piece is built (the repo's own pattern, kept)

1. **Closed tables, access inside functions.** Tables are created with row level security on and every grant revoked from `anon` and `authenticated`. The app reads and writes only through `security definer` functions (`set search_path = public`) that work out who may do what from the caller's **current** state on every call (as the staff chat does), so revoking someone cuts access at once and a guessed id reaches nothing. Plain `create`, never `create or replace`; guarded where a live function is replaced.
2. **A SQL test per migration** (`tests/db/tests/4xx_*.sql`) on the scratch Postgres: who may, who may not, every refusal sentence, the limits, races where money or counts are involved. Small wrong edits to the migration are tried to see that a test notices (`tests/db/tools/mutate_migrations.py`).
3. **The app** gets models that drop bad rows, a service that throws one plain sentence, providers, screens, and tests against the pretend website. **Drift tests read the migration** (function names, arguments, whitelists, limits) so the app cannot silently disagree with the database.
4. **Nothing is applied to production, deployed, built or merged without an explicit yes.** Migrations are written, tested on the scratch database, documented and queued; each batch gets a pre-flight and post-flight like the earlier ones. Until then the app is tested only against the pretend website. Real-device testing needs the migrations applied to a project the phone talks to: that is the owner's go/no-go at the end.
5. **Features ship dark.** A public `site_feature_flags` table (all off at first) gates every feature the PDF says still needs a decision (money, subscriptions, rankings, split payments...). The app asks which are on and hides the rest. The CMS gets a Features page. "Complete" then means built, tested and switchable; **launch** is the owner's switch, after the decisions on p16 of the PDF.

## Decisions I am making so work can continue (change any of them; each says what changing costs)

## What M0 and M1 settled (27 Oct)

- **Database** (website branch `feat/social-backend`, migrations `20261101100000` and `20261101110000`, tests `480` and `490`: 27 + 181 assertions; the whole database suite passes, 4188 assertions; 49 mutants of the two migrations, all caught). Nothing applied anywhere.
- **Usernames are one list** (`site_handles`, a primary key) for people and businesses; a trigger keeps it equal to `profiles.username`, so `save_my_profile` and the old username check keep working, and a business can never take a person's name (or the reverse), even at the same moment. Business usernames do not change. The pre-existing usernames are backfilled by the migration (a test proves it).
- **Who acts as a profile** is worked out on every call: the login for a personal profile or a professional's business profile; for a venue profile the business's owner/organizer or that venue's manager; for an event group the business's owner/organizer. A suspended profile cannot be acted as and is hidden from others.
- **Private accounts** show header and counts only; the follow becomes a request; turning public accepts the waiting requests; turning private keeps existing followers. **Blocking** is between two profiles (not two logins), removes the follows, and the refusal never says "blocked". Following is slowed to 200 new follows an hour.
- **Left for later, on purpose:** profile photos and covers by upload (M2 builds media storage; until then a photo is the existing one or an https link), a venue's profile is created by its business in BottlesUp Business (a screen for that is part of M8), team members for a professional's profile (M8 adds them and extends `profile_is_mine`), blocking across a person's other profiles (M5 with reports and suspension), a CMS page for the switches (M5).
- **App** (branch `feat/social-app`, 595 tests, analyzer unchanged at 6): everything is behind `social`; with the switch off the app looks exactly as before.

## What M2 settled (27 Oct)

- **Database** (website branch `feat/social-backend`, migration `20261102100000_social_posts.sql`, test `500_social_posts.sql` 300 assertions; whole suite 4497; 110 mutants, 5 documented as equivalent). Who sees a post is computed from the viewer's current standing on every read (public / followers / Members Only = on the author's list AND following now); a post one may not see is simply absent. Media is a private bucket `social-media`; its storage policies ask the same rule, so a guessed path reaches nothing. Files go up first into `<profile id>/<name>`, then `post_create` checks each exists in storage with the right type and size (image 10 MB, video 100 MB; the 60 seconds is as the phone reports it). Community-rules acceptance, banned terms (CMS-managed list, empty to start), 20 posts an hour, 20 comments a minute, and reels / stories / Members Only each refuse until their switch is on.
- **App** (branch `feat/social-app`, 757 tests): home feed with stories row and a "Suggested for you" block, post card, post page with comments and replies, create (post up to 10 photos, reel, story; audience; tags to people, events and venues), story viewer, reels viewer, Members Only list, saved posts, profile grid. Pictures are signed together in one request per screen.
- **Not done, on purpose, with where it lands:** camera capture and video posters (needs camera/microphone strings and store answers; library only for now); sharing a post (in-app share is M4's inbox; outside the app needs a website page); report / hide on a post (M5); the community rules text from the CMS (the app shows a draft until M5 adds the CMS field); **removing a deleted account's files from storage and the orphan sweep** (the database cannot delete a file: `delete-account` and a scheduled job must call the storage API; M12); **the iOS privacy manifest, the in-app data list and `DATA_COLLECTED_USER_APP.md` must add user content (posts, comments) and be re-checked when all social data is known (M12)**; pictures and videos are shown at the size they were uploaded (no thumbnails, no transcoding).

## What M3 settled (27 Oct)

- **Database** (website branch `feat/social-backend`, migration `20261103100000_explore_reviews.sql`, test `510_explore_reviews.sql`, mutants `tests/db/tools/mutants_explore_reviews.py`). Venue facts (amenities from a fixed list, parking as the venue states it, minimum age and ID, last entry, kitchen hours, menu link) are plain columns of `site_venues` changed only through `save_venue_facts()` by the venue's owner or manager. A **review** is by a person who actually went (a paid table booking on a night that is over, or a paid ticket to an event that is over, matched by e-mail), one per person per venue or event, editable and deletable; the venue's own profile replies once. Reviews need the community rules accepted, the banned-terms check, 10 a day, and the `reviews` switch. "Hot" venues and events are counted from what really happened in the last two weeks and return only an order and a score. A professionals directory by category, city and name.
- **App**: Explore (search across events, venues and people; date, category, price and availability filters; hot), venue facts and ratings on the venue page and venue cards, reviews list and write screen, the professionals directory.

## What M4 settled (27 Oct)

- **Database** (website branch `feat/social-backend`, migration `20261104100000_inbox_notifications.sql`, test `520_inbox_notifications.sql`; whole suite 4811 assertions; 92 mutants of the migration, all caught). A conversation member is a **profile**, so a business conversation is read by whoever may act as that business right now. Direct, group (up to 50, named, admins add/remove/rename, anyone leaves or mutes) and **booking** conversations (the customer and the venue's own profile; a card shows venue, night, table and party size, never the confirmation code or any payment). **Requests**: a message to someone who does not follow you lands in their Requests box; the writer may send three before an answer; replying accepts; declining hides it quietly. A message is 1 to 2000 characters or a shared post, event, venue or booking with a note; a shared post shows only to people who may see it. 30 messages a minute, retries with the same client key are one message, banned terms and the rules acceptance apply. **Notifications** are written by triggers (follow, request, accepted, like, comment, reply, tag, review reply), never for yourself or between a blocked pair, and are read through the profile they are for. `notifications_purge` deletes read notifications older than 90 days (never fewer than 7); nothing runs it on a timer yet, so it must be scheduled with the go-live bundle (M12), the same way as the chat purge in `supabase/go-live/schedule_chat_purge.sql`. `dm_conversation` reads one conversation's head so a screen does not page the inbox for it.
- **App** (branch `feat/social-app`, about 925 tests; 62 app mutants of the new code, all caught after tests were added for the 8 that first survived): Messages (Messages and Requests boxes, refreshes every 15 s, bell with a count), conversation screen (newest at the bottom, checks every 5 s, long-press to remove your own message, accept or decline a request, a message that did not go stays in the box and a retry is the same message), new message and new group, group details (rename, add, remove, mute, leave), notifications (opening the list marks them read), a Send button on public posts, a Message button on profiles, Message the venue on a paid booking, a Messages button with a count on the profile tab. Everything is behind the `inbox` switch (and `social`).
- **Found by the tests while building it:** the rename dialog disposed its text box while the dialog was still closing (fixed); Leave group went back to the inbox even when the website refused (fixed).
- **Not done, on purpose, with where it lands:** push notifications (needs the owner's Firebase account; the in-app list is what exists), message attachments other than shares, reactions and read receipts, reporting a message or a conversation (M5), scheduling `notifications_purge` (M12), a screen in BottlesUp Business for venue staff to read a venue's conversations (the database already lets whoever may act as the venue read them; whether and where the Business app shows them is for you to decide, not decided yet).

## Order of the rest (changed 27 Oct)

The social core comes first so it can be tried as one thing: **M5 safety, M11 the shell, M12 the whole-app pass**, then the money and marketplace parts **M6 to M10**, which all need decisions from the owner (Stripe, commissions, subscriptions, legal texts).

## What stays the owner's (cannot be built around)

The Apple and Google accounts and the store forms; Firebase (push); the Stripe account choices (Connect or not, subscription prices and tax); the legal texts (community rules, terms, privacy) and the lawyer's review; final pricing and commission; who staffs the moderation queue (the 24-hour review target the stores expect); the decisions on p16 of the PDF; applying each migration batch to production and deploying the edge functions.

## Rules for myself while doing this

- Update this table and `HANDOFF.md` after each milestone; commit and push the branches (never `main`).
- A milestone is not "Built" until its SQL test passes on the scratch database, the app tests pass, and the drift tests read the migration.
- Everything that touches money is tested for a repeated call, a failed payment and a refund path before it counts.
- No builds, no production changes, no new accounts. Say plainly what is not verified.
