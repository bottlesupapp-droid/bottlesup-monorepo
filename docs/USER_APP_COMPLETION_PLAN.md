# Completing the customer app: the plan, the decisions made, and the order of work (27 Oct 2026)

The owner asked (27 Oct) to complete the customer app **100% against the client's plan** (`BottlesUp_User_App_Plan_and_Flows.pdf`, 15 Oct) first, and only then test on real devices. This file is the single plan for that. What exists today and what each section of the PDF needs is in `apps/bottles-up-user/docs/USER_APP_PLAN_STATUS.md`; this file says **how it gets built**.

Production has no social, messaging, marketplace or growth tables, so almost everything below starts in the database. Work happens in the **website repo** (migrations, SQL tests, edge functions, the CMS), then the **customer app**, then the **vendor app** where a screen of its own is needed.

## How every piece is built (the repo's own pattern, kept)

1. **Closed tables, access inside functions.** Tables are created with row level security on and every grant revoked from `anon` and `authenticated`. The app reads and writes only through `security definer` functions (`set search_path = public`) that work out who may do what from the caller's **current** state on every call (as the staff chat does), so revoking someone cuts access at once and a guessed id reaches nothing. Plain `create`, never `create or replace`; guarded where a live function is replaced.
2. **A SQL test per migration** (`tests/db/tests/4xx_*.sql`) on the scratch Postgres: who may, who may not, every refusal sentence, the limits, races where money or counts are involved. Small wrong edits to the migration are tried to see that a test notices (`tests/db/tools/mutate_migrations.py`).
3. **The app** gets models that drop bad rows, a service that throws one plain sentence, providers, screens, and tests against the pretend website. **Drift tests read the migration** (function names, arguments, whitelists, limits) so the app cannot silently disagree with the database.
4. **Nothing is applied to production, deployed, built or merged without an explicit yes.** Migrations are written, tested on the scratch database, documented and queued; each batch gets a pre-flight and post-flight like the earlier ones. Until then the app is tested only against the pretend website. Real-device testing needs the migrations applied to a project the phone talks to: that is the owner's go/no-go at the end.
5. **Features ship dark.** A public `site_feature_flags` table (all off at first) gates every feature the PDF says still needs a decision (money, subscriptions, rankings, split payments...). The app asks which are on and hides the rest. The CMS gets a Features page. "Complete" then means built, tested and switchable; **launch** is the owner's switch, after the decisions on p16 of the PDF.

## Decisions I am making so work can continue (change any of them; each says what changing costs)

|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | A **profile** is separate from the login. A personal profile belongs to one login; a business profile belongs to an organization (`site_organizations`) and optionally one venue. One login switches between the profiles it owns or manages (roles with content rights). | The PDF: "one login, switch between profiles", and BottlesUp Business shares records and permissions. Reuses the staff roles. | A single profile per login would be simpler but cannot do business profiles or team access. |
| D2 | Usernames: 3 to 30 of `a-z 0-9 . _`, unique ignoring case, reserved words blocked. Personal accounts may be **private** (approved followers only); business profiles are always public. | PDF p3 and p4. | |
| D3 | **Media** in a private Supabase Storage bucket; a picture or video is readable only by someone who may see its post (the storage policy asks the same function the feed asks). Transcoding is a service of its own and a cost the client has not agreed. | Public buckets are simpler but would expose Members Only and private-account media by address (the profile-picture bucket already does this). |
| D4 | **Feed**: posts from profiles you follow, newest first, plus a short "suggested" block of popular public posts. No machine ranking. Stories last 24 hours. | "Hot" needs activity counts, not a recommender. | |
| D5 | **Members Only**: an audience on each post, from a per-profile list of members who must already follow the profile. A shared link still checks access. Removing a member ends access at once; no notification either way (PDF p5). | PDF p5. | |
| D6 | **Safety is part of the build, not a later extra**: report (post, comment, profile, message), block, hide/remove by BottlesUp staff from a CMS queue, a banned-words check at post time, and recorded acceptance of community rules. The community-rules and terms **text is a draft for the client and their lawyer to replace**. | Apple (1.2) and Google require this for any app with user-generated content; the PDF p14 lists reporting, blocking and moderation. | Cannot be dropped if posts or messages exist. |
| D7 | **Inbox**: new customer conversations (direct, group, booking) with message requests from non-followers, in new tables (the staff chat stays as is). Polling while a chat is open (as the staff chat does), no realtime socket. A shared booking card shows a safe summary only. | PDF p5. Realtime can replace polling later without changing the tables. | |
| D8 | **Push notifications** are in-app first (a notifications list fed by the same events); push delivery needs the owner's Firebase account and Apple push capability and is wired last. | Cannot be done without those accounts. | |
| D9 | **Professionals' payments**: the customer pays BottlesUp on Stripe Checkout (deposit or full, balance before a deadline); BottlesUp **holds** the money and records the provider's payout in the existing payout ledger approach (BottlesUp makes the transfer, as it does for venues). **No Stripe Connect** until the client chooses it. Commission 5% (0% for a subscribed business), kept in `site_content`. | The PDF's "selected payment model" and the way venue payouts already work. | Connect would pay providers directly but needs onboarding, Stripe approval and a different dispute model. |
| D10 | **Subscriptions** (CAD 29.99 a month or 299 a year, "final approval required") by Stripe Checkout in subscription mode; kept separate from verification (PDF p13). Off until approved. | PDF p13. | |
| D11 | **Ranks** (Verified, Elite 100K, Icon 1M, Legend 10M, Global Icon 50M, BottlesUp Immortal 100M followers) are computed from followers who count as genuine (not blocked, not suspended, not a business-run account follow loop) and awarded by a review step, never bought. Awards, plaques and addresses are stored apart. | PDF p14. | |
| D12 | Venue facts the PDF wants and the database lacks (amenities, parking, age rules, last entry, menus beyond bottles) become **venue columns and lists edited by the venue's owner or manager** like the existing venue setup, shown read-only in the customer app. Parking is shown only as the venue states it, never as live availability. | PDF p6. | |
| D13 | **A block is between people, not single profiles.** The person behind a personal profile and the professional profiles they own (a DJ's, a photographer's) count as one; a venue's or an event group's profile is run by a team and is blocked, and blocks, on its own. Unblocking from any profile lifts it. | Otherwise a blocked person just switches to their other profile. | A per-profile block is one line in `profile_key()`; the blocked list and follows then act per profile again. |
| D14 | **Suspending a person suspends every profile that login owns.** The login keeps working (tickets and bookings are not touched). Restoring brings all of them back. | Same reason as D13, and a suspended DJ profile with a live personal one is no suspension. | |
| D15 | **Nothing is removed automatically.** A number of reports is a reason to look, not a verdict: only a BottlesUp admin removes, suspends or dismisses, and every choice is in the audit log with a note. | Three accounts could otherwise take anyone's post down. | Auto-hiding after N distinct reporters is a small change to the queue function; say the number. |
| D16 | The **community rules text and version live in the CMS**. Until BottlesUp publishes its own, the app shows its draft and the version is 1; publishing makes the next version and everyone accepts again before they post, comment or send. | Apple and Google expect rules a person has agreed to; the client's lawyer writes them. | |
| D17 | A one-to-one conversation with someone who is **blocked or suspended is closed**: not listed, not counted, not readable by its address, not written to. Groups carry on without that person's access. | A suspended or blocked person must not stay reachable through an old conversation. | |

## What M0 and M1 settled (27 Oct)

- **Database** (website branch `feat/social-backend`, migrations `20261101100000` and `20261101110000`, tests `480` and `490`: 27 + 181 assertions; the whole database suite passes, 4188 assertions; 49 mutants of the two migrations, all caught). Nothing applied anywhere.
- **Usernames are one list** (`site_handles`, a primary key) for people and businesses; a trigger keeps it equal to `profiles.username`, so `save_my_profile` and the old username check keep working, and a business can never take a person's name (or the reverse), even at the same moment. Business usernames do not change. The pre-existing usernames are backfilled by the migration (a test proves it).
- **Who acts as a profile** is worked out on every call: the login for a personal profile or a professional's business profile; for a venue profile the business's owner/organizer or that venue's manager; for an event group the business's owner/organizer. A suspended profile cannot be acted as and is hidden from others.
- **Private accounts** show header and counts only; the follow becomes a request; turning public accepts the waiting requests; turning private keeps existing followers. **Blocking** is between two profiles (not two logins), removes the follows, and the refusal never says "blocked". Following is slowed to 200 new follows an hour.
- **Left for later, on purpose:** profile photos and covers by upload (M2 builds media storage; until then a photo is the existing one or an https link), a venue's profile is created by its business in BottlesUp Business (a screen for that is part of M8), team members for a professional's profile (M8 adds them and extends `profile_is_mine`), blocking across a person's other profiles (M5 with reports and suspension), a CMS page for the switches (M5).
- **App** (branch `feat/social-app`, 595 tests, analyzer unchanged at 6): everything is behind `social`; with the switch off the app looks exactly as before.

## What M2 settled (27 Oct)

## What M4 settled (27 Oct)

## What M11 settled (27 Oct)

- **The layout** (customer app, branch `feat/social-app`): with the `social` switch on, the tabs are **Home, Explore, a Create button, Inbox (with a count of what is new), Profile**; with it off the four original tabs (Events, Tables, Bookings, Profile) are exactly as before. - **Not done, on purpose**: a push-notification switch (needs Firebase), a settings page for who may message you (requests already handle strangers), picture-based caption ideas (would send the picture to a third party).
